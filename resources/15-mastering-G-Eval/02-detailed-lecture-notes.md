### G-Eval Innovation 2: Probability-Weighted Scoring

Now comes the next innovation of G-Eval. If you remember, what were we saying before? Simply generate a score between zero and 10. It could be 8, it could be 7, it could be 6. And because it was generating a single integer, the chance of variance coming in was very high.

What does G-Eval do? It solves this problem with the help of a **probability-weighted score**. Okay? How does it work? Let me tell you.

For this you need to know how any LLM or transformer generates output. So the final layer of your network has as many nodes as you have output tokens. Right? So suppose you have 10,000 output tokens. It'll contain everything, all the words, all the digits, everything. And what will your model do? Based on the input, it will assign a probability to every token. Suppose this token is "is," this is "the," this is "zero," this is "hello." Basically there are many such tokens, and your LLM assigns a probability to every token.

Suppose it assigned this one a probability of 61, this one a probability of 31. This is how you assign probabilities to tokens, and whichever has the highest probability among these, that token gets printed in that particular cycle. Then it gets added into the input, and then this whole process repeats the same way. Then again all tokens' output probabilities exist, whichever has the maximum, that word comes out next time. So this is auto, auto-regressive, right? What's the term? What's the term? Auto-regressive manner, right, this is called. In an auto-regressive manner your output keeps getting printed.

Now think for yourself: if my prompt tells me, "give me a score from 0 to 10," analyze this whole thing and then assign me a score between zero and 10, then obviously even though we have 10,000 tokens, which tokens will get assigned the highest probabilities? Think about it: these tokens 0, 1, 2, 3, up to 10. There are 10,000 tokens I have. Output token, we're assigning a probability to every token. But since my prompt is explicitly telling me that I need to state a number in the output, between 0 and 10, then obviously, whichever bigger probabilities get assigned, they'll be assigned to these numbers. Is this point clear? And the rest, the other different words, "is," "the," and all those, will get assigned lower probabilities. Is the point clear up to here?
